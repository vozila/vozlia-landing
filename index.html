The problem

The haircut isn't the first problem. The wait is.

Every other part of life got faster and more digital. The barbershop walk-in didn't — it's still a clipboard, a crowded lobby, and a guess at how long you'll wait.

51%
of barbershop customers say waiting around is their biggest in-store frustration.
Zenoti, 2023 (barbershop segment)
77%
of consumers cite convenience as a key factor in their choices — and will pay more for it.
Morgan Stanley, AlphaWise Consumer Trends 2024

Appointments went digital. Walk-ins didn't.

But the wait isn't just an annoyance to smooth over — it's the exact moment a new customer decides whether to try you at all.

Why the walk-in matters

A walk-in is a new customer, deciding.

They don't know your barbers. They don't know your wait. They don't know whether walking in will cost them 20 minutes or an hour.

So show them.

1

See the wait

The live wait time is on the window before they even walk in — no guessing, no walking away.

2

Join the line

Scan a QR code and hold your spot from your phone. No app to download, no phone call to make.

3

Go about your day

No crowded lobby. Grab a coffee, run an errand — your spot is held while you wait wherever you want.

4

Get notified when you're up

NextUp texts them the moment they're next, so they head back with time to spare.

5

Get in the chair

Their name comes up on the board and the station tablet. The barber calls them; the chair keeps moving.

Make the wait visible, and the sidewalk becomes your front door.

Everywhere they look

One live queue. Every surface.

You just saw it on the window and the wall. The same live queue reaches every screen you already have — right down to the barber's station. No proprietary hardware to buy.

Mirror / station

Routes them to the barber

At the chair, it tells the barber who's up and which station — so calling the next customer is effortless.

The merchant upside

Keep the customer in your line — even when they're not in your shop.

A physical line loses people twice: some won't join when it looks long, and some give up after waiting too long. A digital queue lets a customer hold their spot and step out — grab a coffee, run an errand, wait in the car — then come back when they're up. So you can hold demand that would otherwise walk out the door, without needing everyone crammed into the waiting area.

A queue they can leave without losing their place addresses both.
